**Players** gathers the games of anyone (you, your opponents or your students) and shows you how they play. It is also where you prepare a game against someone in particular.

## Creating a profile

There are two ways to start:

- **Search players in your databases:** find anyone across all your local databases. Names can be searched in any order and without accents.
- **Load from Lichess or Chess.com:** download any user's games and open their profile.

Then save the profile as yours, an opponent's, a student's or another player's. You can merge several records of the same player and add games from other sources: a PGN or Lichess's official broadcasts of over-the-board tournaments.

## The profile

- **Summary:** games, score, rating history, activity and results by color and time control.
- **Games:** the filterable list, to open, analyze or export them.
- **Openings:** grouped by family, variation and sub-variation.
- **Rivals:** who they face most, their nemesis and their most favorable opponent.
- **Analysis:** engine analysis of their games: mistakes by phase of the game, habits and pawn structures.

Every results table shows the **rating par**: it compares the score with what the rating differences predict, so you can tell whether 38% with a line is a lot or a little.

On your own profile, the **Training** tab turns your mistakes into exercises (see [Training](../training/)).

## Preparing against an opponent

The **Preparation** tab gathers what you need for a game against that person:

- **How to play:** the lines, structures and types of game where they score below expectation, and a verdict. It is guidance, not a certainty: if nothing stands out, it tells you to play your repertoire.
- **Likely repertoire:** their move tree with an interactive board and their recent deviations.
- **Key positions:** the positions in their lines where they are most likely to go wrong.
- **Against my repertoire:** the opponent's replies your repertoire does not answer yet.
- **Twin bot:** a bot that plays their real repertoire and, outside it, like a player of their level and style. Use it to rehearse the game.

From here you can also generate lines and find surprises with their games, and export the whole preparation as PGN.

The more games a profile has, the more reliable all of this is. With few games, Onyx always shows the sample size.
